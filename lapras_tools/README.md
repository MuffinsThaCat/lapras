## Where things stand (read this first; the sections below are the evidence, the audit history is the chronology)

Every claim below was tested against external data. Later passes sometimes overturned earlier ones; this summary reflects the latest state (the "Twentieth pass" in the audit history).

| Component | What it does | What the evidence supports | What it does NOT support |
|---|---|---|---|
| D line (3.03) | modular vs global verdict per disease | verdicts are stable under data noise for 25 of 31 diseases (5 modular, 19-20 global; 6-7 unstable: atherosclerosis, obesity, NAFLD, CKD, Parkinson's, osteoporosis, + Huntington's marginal) | prediction of whether combination therapy is needed: AUC 0.82 overall comes entirely from cancers (non-cancer 0.60); three independent measures (my labels, co-formulated products, label-mined combination pairs) agree |
| Combination Designer | proposes drugs per module (`spread`) or linked pairs (`vertical`) | runs on a 1,503-drug ChEMBL-verified library with indications, marketed status and equally-eligible alternatives; picks are approved-for-disease drugs far more often than before | that "one drug per module" or "linked pairs" is how real combinations are built: approved partners are only marginally more linked than other same-disease drugs on a disease-agnostic network (absent at STRING 0.4/0.7, marginal at 0.9) |
| Patient tool | reweights a disease network by a patient's expression, reranks targets, proposes a plan | with cohort-relative weights it tracks real subtype biology (ESR1 rank 2 vs 18-22 in ER+ vs ER-); expression-eligibility filter (0.25) removes ER drugs from ER-negative tumours | any added information beyond the expression itself: bill AUC 0.949 vs weights 0.939 (breast ER status, 260 tumours), 0.960 vs 0.954 (lung histology, 233); per-patient D is not informative |
| Interaction tool | network-position flag + CYP/P-gp pharmacokinetic flag | vs DDInter (independent): network AUC 0.52 (any interaction) / 0.60 (Major); PK flag 1.5x / 2x lift; together CV AUC 0.60 for Major | clinical use: weak everywhere, absence of a flag means nothing |
| Ecosystem, finance, pandemic (`coupling_other_fields/`) | the same machinery on other networks | pandemic: with cancelled travel on real US passenger flows the bill ties strength/degree/betweenness and beats random closure | any advantage over simple degree/strength rules; ecosystem bill does not beat degree on 3 real webs; finance bill does not beat plain coupling strength |

Nothing here is a clinical or policy tool. The validation harnesses (`validate_*.py`) are the most durable product: each states its ground truth, its controls and its limits.

## Hardening round (2026-11-11, after the user agreed to harden tools 1 and 2)

### 1. D has error bars: only 5 of the 9 "modular" diseases are robustly modular
`uncertainty.py` drops 10% of edges at random and multiplies weights by lognormal(0, 0.1) noise, 100 times per disease (a sensitivity analysis, not a fitted error model; the perturbation also moves D on its own, in both directions: heart failure 2.10 -> median 2.34, schizophrenia 2.03 -> 1.60, so the 5-95% interval need not contain the unperturbed D). `python3 -m lapras_tools.uncertainty --sweep` checks the verdicts are not an artefact of these settings: six (edge-drop, noise) settings x two seeds. At the default 60 draws per setting the same six diseases are unstable and the other 25 never change verdict (5 modular, 20 global); with 200 draws per setting Huntington's also joins the unstable set (P(global) 0.92-0.99, dipping under the 0.9 cutoff for some seed/setting combinations), so the stricter count is 5 modular and 19 global robust, 7 unstable. Verdicts for diseases sitting near the 0.1/0.9 cutoffs are themselves sensitive to the number of draws (a `--n-sweep 20` run flags 7 as well). Edge dropout alone pushes the borderline modular-side diseases toward modular (e.g. NAFLD P(global) 0.02, obesity 0.17, CKD 0.19), weight noise alone toward global (NAFLD 0.29, obesity 0.67, CKD 0.79): for those, the verdict is genuinely on the fence. NAFLD leans modular (P(global) 0.02-0.29 across settings) and osteoporosis leans global (0.57-0.93).

- Robustly MODULAR (>=90% of perturbations below 3.03): epilepsy, depression, schizophrenia, heart failure, sickle cell.
- UNCERTAIN (straddle the line): atherosclerosis (P(global)=0.50), obesity (0.46), NAFLD/NASH (0.19), chronic kidney disease (0.71), Parkinson's (0.58), osteoporosis (0.74).
- Robustly GLOBAL: 20 of 22 (all cancers, RA, T1D/T2D, asthma, Crohn's, HIV, TB, lupus, MS, IPF, Huntington's, scleroderma).
- So the main README's "9 modular / 22 global" holds for 5 / 20; four of the nine modular claims (atherosclerosis, obesity, NAFLD, CKD) and Parkinson's/osteoporosis sit on the line within plausible data error. The top-10 bill list is more stable (mean overlap with the unperturbed top-10: 0.78-0.95).

### 2. Drug library is now ChEMBL-derived, with indications
`build_druglib.py` re-derives targets, action, max phase and indications for all 167 library drugs from ChEMBL (resolving parent molecules; mechanisms hang off salt forms). Audit of my hand-written seed: after fixing my own fetcher bugs, 19 genuine discrepancies remain: ChEMBL records some drugs against narrower targets than their pharmacology (everolimus/sirolimus only FKBP1A; cabozantinib lacks RET/AXL; lenvatinib lacks FGFR/KIT/RET; imatinib lacks PDGFRA), a few real corrections (valproate -> ALDH5A1, levodopa -> DRD3, dolutegravir/tenofovir -> viral POL), insulin and tenofovir have lower ChEMBL phases than I assumed, and TTI-101 is not in ChEMBL. Policy: targets = ChEMBL targets plus the seed-only targets (kept, flagged "NOT in ChEMBL mechanism data" in the output). Remaining limits: the seed list of 167 drugs is still my selection; ChEMBL's mechanism annotation is conservative and for some drugs incomplete; indications are ChEMBL phases, not guidelines.
The Combination Designer now: prefers a protein with a drug indicated for the disease (ChEMBL phase >= 3), shows the matched indication and phase ("APPROVED for Epilepsy" vs "phase 3 trials for X; not approved for this"), flags mechanism-only picks, and counts modules covered by multi-target drugs. `--min-phase 4` restricts to approved indications. Example (approved only): glioblastoma -> dabrafenib (BRAF) + trametinib (MAP2K1) + vorasidenib (IDH1) + eprenetapopt as a mechanism-only TP53 pick.

**Bulk library (built 2026-11-11).** `build_druglib_full.py` pages through ChEMBL's mechanism table (7,561 rows) and keeps every drug with a named parent molecule, max phase >= 3 and at least one target on a Lapras network protein: 1,460 drugs (1,079 approved), with indications. `default_library()` merges it with the verified 167 and the hand-written seed: 1,503 drugs, 1,502 ChEMBL-verified (the one unverified is TTI-101). The 43 seed drugs the bulk file lacks (olaparib, everolimus, letrozole...) have targets that are not on any network protein, and the merge restores them. The larger library fixes the epilepsy case: felbamate (approved NMDA antagonist) / carbamazepine / ezogabine instead of ketamine. Caveat: with 16 approved EGFR drugs and 31 on ESR1, which one is shown is a tie-break (most selective, then alphabetical); each pick now prints the equally eligible alternatives, and is not a recommendation.

### 3. Patient-specific tool on real RNA-seq (260 TCGA-BRCA primary tumours, real receptor status)
`python3 -m lapras_tools.validate_tcga --fetch 260` downloads the tumours (open-access GDC) and `python3 -m lapras_tools.validate_tcga` reruns everything below. Two weighting schemes, first compared on expression-defined groups (ESR1-high n=89 vs triple-negative-like n=69):

| | transcriptome percentile (original) | cohort-relative (recommended, leave-one-out) |
|---|---|---|
| patient D mean +/- sd, range | 4.13 +/- 0.11, [3.86, 4.51] | 3.82 +/- 0.29, [2.91, 4.37] |
| shuffled-control D | 4.08 +/- 0.14 | 3.86 +/- 0.27 |
| real vs shuffled D (KS) | p < 0.001 (a 0.05 shift) | p = 0.37 (indistinguishable) |
| ESR1 bill rank, ESR1-high / TN-like | median 2 / 4 | median 2 / 19 |
| top-10 bill-set Jaccard within / across groups | 0.91, 0.91 / 0.90 | 0.42, 0.36 / 0.27 |

Then with REAL ER status from the TCGA clinical table (248 tumours: 176 ER+, 72 ER-negative, 32 triple-negative by IHC), cohort-relative weights:
- ESR1's bill rank separates ER+ from ER-negative at AUC 0.94 (median rank 2 vs 18); raw ESR1 expression alone gives 0.97.
- **The network adds nothing beyond expression for this task.** Cross-validated logistic regression predicting ER status: the 47 expression weights give AUC 0.939, the 47 bill values 0.949 (a wash). ER+ vs triple-negative only: weights 0.965, bill 0.946.
- Patient D alone separates ER status at AUC 0.79 (ER+ higher: 3.91 vs 3.60, p ~ 1e-12): a real group-level effect, but per-patient D is indistinguishable from the shuffled control, and 98% of tumours stay above the 3.03 line.
- Survival: 21 deaths in 248 (short follow-up, underpowered); D dead 3.92 vs alive 3.81, p = 0.04, uncorrected and confounded with ER status. Not evidence of prognostic value.

Conclusions: whole-transcriptome weighting barely personalises (top-10 lists ~91% identical across subtypes), so `--reference` is recommended and the tool warns without it; with it the rankings track real biology, but they carry no information the expression did not already have, and per-patient "D = 3.2 vs 4.5" is not supported. The designer used to propose fulvestrant for an ER-negative tumour; targets whose expression weight is below `--min-expression` (default 0.25) are now ineligible. Not done: other cancers, PAM50 subtypes, treatment-response data.

### 4. Retrospective test of the D = 3.03 line (`validate_line.py`): weak
Ground truth: does the first-line guideline regimen combine >= 2 drugs of different mechanism (label 1) or is it a single agent (label 0)? Labels were written by Claude from general knowledge of guidelines in one pass before comparing to D, not adjudicated by a clinician; 7 ambiguous diseases are excluded (reason printed); edit `LABELS` to test other calls.
- All 24 labelled diseases (12 combination / 12 single-agent): D > 3.03 predicts "combination" with accuracy 0.67 (majority-class 0.50; Fisher p = 0.19); AUC 0.82 (permutation p = 0.003). Misclassified: IPF, Huntington's, osteoporosis, type 2 diabetes, type 1 diabetes and MS (global but single-agent first-line) and heart failure, CKD (modular but combination first-line).
- **The signal is the cancers.** Cancers are all global AND all combination-treated. Non-cancer only (17 diseases): AUC 0.60 (p = 0.28), accuracy 0.53 vs 0.71 majority-class. "Is it a cancer?" alone scores AUC 0.79; raw edge count in the STRING network scores 0.88 (cancers are the best-studied diseases, so more literature-derived edges: a likely confound). Total correlation scores 0.44.
- Restricting to diseases whose verdict is robust to data noise (n=20): AUC 0.84, accuracy 0.70, Fisher p = 0.13.
- **Independent cross-check not based on my labels (tenth pass).** For each disease, the number of approved fixed-dose products (Drugs@FDA) whose two ingredients are BOTH phase-4 indicated for it: type 2 diabetes 14, asthma 5, heart failure 4, atherosclerosis 3, everything else <= 1. Spearman(D, count) = -0.29 over all 31 diseases (p = 0.11; non-cancer -0.15, p = 0.50), so this second opinion does not support D either, and it agrees poorly with my labels (AUC 0.42), mostly because type 2 diabetes (14 co-formulated products; my label "single-agent: metformin first-line") is a genuinely disputable call. Co-formulation reflects commercial packaging as well as guidelines, so it is a weak proxy.
- Sensitivity to my labels (built into `validate_line.py`): flipping any single label keeps the all-disease AUC in 0.74-0.88 and the non-cancer AUC in 0.47-0.71. Including the 7 ambiguous diseases under the label most favourable to D gives AUC 0.82 (non-cancer 0.71); under the least favourable, 0.68 (non-cancer 0.44). The "weak outside oncology" conclusion does not depend on a handful of disputed labels.
So the claim "above the line, combinations are required" is not supported outside oncology; "below the line, one drug suffices" is right 6 of 8 times (misses: heart failure, CKD).

### 5. Do approved combinations hit different modules? (`validate_combos.py`): not clearly
Test of the Combination Designer's premise (one drug per module). Eight distinct approved target pairs (BRAF+MEK, alpelisib/capivasertib/palbociclib/abemaciclib + fulvestrant, encorafenib+cetuximab, sotorasib+panitumumab; list written from general knowledge, editable): 5 of 8 sit in the SAME module (random approved-drug pairs on the same networks share a module 40% of the time; binomial p = 0.18, not significant). Scored by the vertical criterion (bill_i + bill_j) * coupling_ij among all coupled approved-drug pairs on the disease network, the approved pairs sit at a median 88th percentile with the 1,503-drug library (86th with the 167-drug one; chance 50%; Wilcoxon p = 0.055, n=8): breast and colorectal pairs rank in the top 3-26%, but BRAF+MEK ranks near the bottom in melanoma and lung (MAP2K1 has a low bill there). Suggestive, not significant. **What does carry signal (eighth-pass audit; NOT controlled for same-disease pairing, which raises the random base rate of linkage ~2.6x: see the confound note in section 7):** 6 of the 8 approved pairs are DIRECTLY LINKED in the network, against 31% of all approved-drug-target pairs (binomial p = 0.014). The coupling *magnitude* in the vertical score is inert: STRING edges >= 0.9 are all ~1, and replacing the score by a plain bill sum (with or without requiring a link) gives the same percentile (median 90-91%, vs 85% for coupling alone); so the vertical score is effectively "bill sum of a linked pair", and with n=8 the enrichment cannot be attributed to coupling rather than to high-bill drugged targets. Because the evidence leans against "one per module", the designer has a second mode, `--mode vertical`, proposing tightly coupled same-module pairs (e.g. breast: palbociclib+fulvestrant first, abemaciclib+fulvestrant fourth); `--mode spread` (default) is the one-per-module design.

### 6. Systematic test of "combination partners sit close in the network" (`validate_fdc.py`)
(Not controlled for same-disease pairing; the matched control in section 7 shrinks linkage enrichment to about 2x.) Every two-ingredient product in Drugs@FDA (4,653 products), ingredients mapped to library drugs: 92 distinct pairs, 64 evaluable (both drugs target the 599-protein union network, no shared target). Raw: 30% of pairs have directly linked targets vs 3% for random pairs from the same drugs (p ~ 1e-15). **That number is inflated by pseudo-replication**: the list is dominated by contraceptives (ethinyl estradiol sits in 11 evaluable pairs, metformin in 9). With each drug used at most once per resample (2,000 resamples, ~25 independent pairs): 20% linked (5-95%: 16-24%) vs 3%, binomial p ~ 4e-4; same module 22% vs 5%. Excluding every product with a hormone-receptor drug (ESR1/PGR/AR/ESR2/NR3C1): 4 of 44 linked = 9% vs 3% (p = 0.045, marginal): fluoxetine+olanzapine, ezetimibe+simvastatin, ertugliflozin+sitagliptin, empagliflozin+linagliptin. Reading: co-formulated partners do sit closer in the network than chance, but outside sex-hormone pairs the effect is small. Together with the oncology result in section 5 (6 of 8 approved pairs linked vs 31%, p = 0.014; disease networks, hand-picked pairs) this is consistent evidence for "linked pairs", not for "one drug per module".

### 7. Large-scale combination validation (`validate_combos_large.py`): approved combination partners are network-linked
**Confound audit (`validate_combos_matched.py`, added after this section was written): the comparison below overstates the effect.** Random pairs come from drugs of *any* disease, but combination partners are mostly drugs for the *same* disease, and the union network is built from per-disease protein sets. Random pairs that merely share a phase-4 indication are directly linked 13.4% of the time vs 5.0% for fully random pairs (2.7x), so same-disease alone explains much of the raw gap. Against the indication-matched null: all 112 evaluable pairs 30% vs 13.4% (p = 7.3e-6); pairs that share an indication 24% (20/84) vs 13.4% (p = 0.007); independent resamples (each drug once, ~34 pairs) 27% vs 13.4% (p = 0.032); pairs that do NOT share a specific indication 46% (13/28) vs 5.0% random. So partner linkage survives the same-disease control but at roughly 2x (not 6x) over base rate, with modest p-values once pseudo-replication is removed. Treat the p-values in the table below as upper bounds on significance. **Disease-agnostic network check (`validate_combos_string.py`):** the union network is built from per-disease protein lists, which is partly circular with drug indications, so the same test was repeated on STRING itself (interactions >= 0.9 among the 254 target genes of the 187 pool drugs, fetched from the STRING API, no disease lists). Approved pairs (155 evaluable) are directly linked 21.9% vs 4.6% for random pool pairs (p = 3e-14) and 12.1% for indication-sharing pairs (p = 4e-4); restricted to pairs that share an indication it is 17.5% vs 12.1% (p = 0.052), and with each drug used once 20% vs 12.1% (p = 0.097). So beyond same-disease pairing the linkage enrichment is about 1.5x and not significant at 0.05 at the strictest threshold; **a sweep (`validate_combos_string.py --sweep`: STRING score 0.4/0.7/0.9 x heading cutoff 20/60/200) shows it is absent at the lenient thresholds** (indication-sharing approved pairs vs indication-sharing pool pairs: 54.8% vs 55.2% at 0.4, 17.2% vs 17.2% at 0.7, 27.5% vs 26.2%; independent-pair p = 0.2-0.4) and at 0.9 only marginal (14.0-17.5% vs 9.5-12.1%, independent p = 0.065-0.097). The strong raw result ("approved pairs are 4-5x more linked than random pairs") is explained by "drugs for the same disease have nearby targets"; there is no robust evidence that combination partners are closer than other drugs for the same disease. Systematic replacement for the 8 hand-picked pairs (section 5) and the FDC-only dataset (section 6). Mines 10,451 openFDA drug labels whose indications contain "in combination with", maps both the label drug and the named partner to the library, and merges with FDC products. Parsing hardened in 14th audit: self-pairs (prodrug/salt forms like olmesartan+olmesartan medoxomil), endogenous molecules (angiotensin II in drug-class descriptions), and negative-context matches ("should not be used with") filtered out. Result: 174 total pairs, 112 evaluable (both drugs have a target on the 599-protein union network, no shared target, 124 distinct drugs).

| Signal | Approved pairs | Random pairs (same pool) | Binomial p |
|--------|---------------|-------------------------|------------|
| Directly linked | 33/112 = 29% | 5% | 2.9e-17 |
| Reachable in 1-2 hops | 51/112 = 46% | 18% | 3.9e-11 |
| Same module (k=40) | 26/112 = 23% | 7% | 1.1e-8 |
| Bill sum (mean) | 0.058 | 0.049 | 0.24 (n.s.) |

**De-duplication.** The most-used drugs are ethinyl estradiol (11 pairs), metformin (9), fulvestrant (7). Independent-set resampling (each drug at most once, ~41 pairs per set): linked 23% (5-95%: 18-28%) vs 5%, binomial p = 0.00013. Partner-shuffle permutation: p = 0.0003. Excluding hormone-receptor drugs (ESR1/PGR/AR/ESR2/NR3C1): 11/77 linked = 14% vs 4%, p = 9e-5.

**What the 2-hop row adds.** 18 approved pairs are reachable in 2 hops but not directly linked: CDK4/6+ESR1 combos (palbociclib/abemaciclib/ribociclib + fulvestrant), cetuximab+encorafenib (EGFR—BRAF), inavolisib/gedatolisib+palbociclib (PIK3CA—CCND1), everolimus+lenvatinib/sunitinib/sorafenib (MTOR—RTK). These are known pathway-coupled combinations that sit one intermediary apart in STRING.

**What is NOT captured (55%).** 61 pairs have no path within 2 hops: chemo+targeted (erlotinib+gemcitabine), IO+TKI (axitinib+pembrolizumab), supportive-care pairings (abiraterone+prednisone), and metabolic combinations (insulin+liraglutide). The combination rationale for these is not pathway proximity, and the framework should not be expected to catch them.

**Interpretation.** All three topology signals (direct link, 2-hop, same module) are highly significant; individual target importance (bill sum) is not. The coupling network identifies combination partners through TOPOLOGY, not through importance of individual targets. This extends the hand-picked result (n=8, p=0.014) to a systematic dataset with p < 1e-16. The ~55% of approved combinations that are not pathway-proximate are a ceiling: the framework identifies one class of combination logic (pathway coupling) among several.

### 8. `--min-expression` sensitivity (`validate_tcga.py --cutoff`)
248 real tumours with ER status; design run at each cutoff:

| cutoff | ER+ given an ESR1 drug | ER-negative given an ESR1 drug | mean modules with no eligible drug | plan Jaccard vs 0.25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 (no filter) | 68% | 29% | 0.50 | 0.78 |
| 0.10 | 68% | 18% | 0.66 | 0.83 |
| **0.25 (default)** | 67% | 7% | 0.94 | 1.00 |
| 0.40 | 64% | 1% | 1.23 | 0.81 |
| 0.50 | 56% | 1% | 1.44 | 0.68 |

Without the filter 29% of ER-negative tumours get an ER-targeting drug; 0.25 cuts that to 7% and keeps 67% of ER+ tumours. 0.25 is defensible but not unique (0.40 reaches 1% for a 3-point recall cost); the plan is stable around it.

## What the other validation runs showed

- **Interactions: practically uninformative (ninth-pass systematic test, `validate_ddi.py`).** Ground truth = every pair of library drugs where one FDA label's Drug Interactions section names the other: 152,345 scorable pairs, 1,826 named (1.2%). AUC of the interaction score = **0.529** (label-shuffled null 0.500 +/- 0.009, z = +3.4: above chance, but negligible). The OVERLAP flag has 2.3% precision (base rate 1.2%, recall 16%); the strictest TIGHT flag 3.8% precision, recall 6.7%, i.e. 96% of its flags are not label-named interactions. Within popularity strata the AUC is 0.54-0.55 for the pairs that carry most positives. The label ground truth is noisy and mostly pharmacokinetic (CYP) interactions, which this tool does not model, so some of the gap is by construction, but nothing here supports using it clinically. **The 0.84-0.89 below came from 7 pairs I hand-picked as pathway-level interactions and was optimistic.**
- **PK layer added (tenth pass, `pk_layer.py`).** The documented gap was "pharmacokinetic (CYP) interactions are not modelled". CYP/P-gp roles (substrate / inhibitor / inducer) are now mined from each drug's own FDA label (284 drugs get a role, 45 a perpetrator role) and the interaction CLI prints enzyme-mediated flags (e.g. fluoxetine inhibits CYP2D6 -> paroxetine). Measured against the same label ground truth: PK flag precision 5.7% vs a 3.1% base rate (1.8x lift), recall 26%, AUC 0.563 (PK count 0.59 cross-validated). On the pairs scorable by both layers, the **network score is at chance (AUC 0.493)** and network + PK (0.575) does worse than PK alone, so the best-supported part of the interaction tool is the PK flag, and it is still weak. Circularity caveat (in `pk_layer.py`): roles come from the same label sections that supply the ground truth, so 0.59 is an upper bound on independent accuracy.
- Earlier 7-pair result (superseded by the line above): AUC 0.89 against random library pairs (0.84 with the 167-drug library), but n=7. The false-flag base rate depends on the null pool: 24.8% of random pairs reached OVERLAP and 4.7% TIGHT with 167 drugs, 8.4% and 2.1% with 1,503 (more drugs on peripheral targets), so the improvement is partly a different null rather than a better tool. Aspirin+rivaroxaban (a real bleeding interaction) is a miss. Direction is only labelled "both suppress / opposing". Pharmacokinetic (CYP) interactions are not modelled.
- **Patient demo (`--demo`, 3 SIMULATED subtypes):** D 4.08 / 4.07 / 4.33 vs generic 4.03; none distinguishable from random reweighting at 50-200 shuffles (p = 0.65 / 0.67 / 0.16 at n=50). Mechanics demo only; the subtypes were written by me.

## Limitations that remain

- The combination designer matches drugs to targets in a network; it knows nothing about dose, safety, resistance, or trial evidence beyond ChEMBL phase. For infections the host network does not contain the viral/bacterial targets of real regimens.
- D is a coarse statistic: its verdict is robust to data noise for 25 of 31 diseases (uncertain for 6), but being robust is not being predictive: against first-line regimens it adds little beyond "is it a cancer?" (section 4).
- No tests written. Files I edited that were yours: `batch.py` (`--uncertainty` flag only) and the main `README.md` (one caveats section). `pipeline.py` and `coupling.py` untouched. A test run of `batch.py` on a subset overwrote `results/periodic_table.txt` and `results/cross_disease.txt`; I regenerated both with the documented full command (sizes and D values verified), and `results/periodic_table.txt` now ends with the robustness section.
