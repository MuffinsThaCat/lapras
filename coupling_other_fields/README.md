# Coupling analysis beyond biology

The Lapras engine (`../lapras_tools/core.py`: bill, hierarchy, D) applied to three non-drug networks.
These were split out of `lapras_tools/` because a drug-discovery user has no use for them; they are
experiments in whether the same machinery says anything useful elsewhere. Run from `drug-discovery/`.

| Tool | Command | Data |
|------|---------|------|
| Ecosystem collapse | `python3 -m coupling_other_fields.ecosystem web.csv` / `--experiment` | your food web (`consumer,resource`); experiment is SYNTHETIC niche-model |
| Pandemic circuit breaker | `python3 -m coupling_other_fields.pandemic` / `--experiment` (`--bts data/db1b_2023_q1.zip` for REAL US passenger volumes, `--population geonames`, `--flows gravity`, `--reroute 0|1`) | real OpenFlights routes (top-300 airports) or `--edges a,b,weight` |
| Financial contagion | `python3 -m coupling_other_fields.finance exposures.csv [banks.csv]` / `--experiment` | your exposures; experiment is SYNTHETIC |

## Measured results (paired, with SE)

- **Ecosystem (synthetic niche webs): yes.** Bill vs single-species secondary extinctions: Spearman +0.51 (degree -0.09, number-of-consumers +0.27; tie-aware ranks). Removing species in bill order collapses half the web after removing 10.6% of species (degree 28.8%, random 22.5%, consumers 10.7%). Paired: bill beats degree by 0.182 +/- 0.019 SE and random by 0.119 +/- 0.013, but is a tie with the consumer count on robustness (-0.002 +/- 0.014); on single-removal correlation it beats consumers by +0.247 +/- 0.027 SE. D separates webs: mean secondary extinctions 0.81 below the line vs 1.46 above (n=28 / 12). Untested on a real food web (none was reachable).
- **Ecosystem, one REAL web (Little Rock Lake, KONECT `maayan-foodweb`; 183 nodes, 2,494 links; data in `data/little_rock_lake.csv`): not confirmed.** `python3 -m coupling_other_fields.ecosystem coupling_other_fields/data/little_rock_lake.csv --evaluate [--theta T]`. Edge orientation: "links represent food sources", read as from-node eats to-node (median 11 prey per consumer, a typical food-web value; the reversed reading gives a median of 1; `data/little_rock_lake_reversed.csv` is provided to check). The dataset has exactly one prey-less node and no basal species after it is treated as an external input, so topological cascades are dominated by cycles; single-removal analysis is degenerate (4% of species cause any secondary extinction at theta = 0.5, 1% at theta = 1). Against 300 random removal orders (single web, so the null is the random orders): at theta = 0.5 the bill ranks 0% of random orders better (z = -1.8), tied with the single-removal-damage order and ahead of degree (z = -1.6); at theta = 1.0 degree (z = -5.3) and number-of-consumers (z = -5.7) clearly beat the bill (z = -2.1). So the bill is better than random on this web but the synthetic-web advantage over degree does not replicate, and the result flips with the extinction threshold. One web, odd structure: weak evidence either way.
- **Ecosystem, three real webs (seventeenth pass): the synthetic-web advantage does not replicate.** Added the two Florida cypress-wetland carbon-exchange webs (KONECT `foodweb-baywet` / `foodweb-baydry`, 128 taxa, 2,106 / 2,137 links; "a taxon uses another taxon as food", same orientation as Little Rock Lake; `data/florida_wetland_wet.csv`, `data/florida_wetland_dry.csv`). The Chesapeake Bay web (39 nodes) is stored undirected, so the extinction model cannot use it. Like Little Rock Lake, these datasets have no basal species once prey-less nodes are treated as external inputs, so cascades are degenerate (removing one species can kill ~125 others). Against 300 random removal orders per web: at theta = 0.5 bill, degree, n-consumers and single-removal damage all tie (0.008, z = -2.1 on both Florida webs). At theta = 1.0: Little Rock Lake bill z = -2.1 vs degree -5.3; Florida wet bill z = +0.2 (no better than random) vs degree -1.9; Florida dry bill +0.2 vs degree -1.8; the single-removal-damage ordering is best on the Florida webs (-2.0). So on none of the three real webs does the bill beat degree, and on the Florida webs it is no better than random at theta = 1. The synthetic niche-web result (bill beat degree by 0.18 robustness) is a property of that generator, not demonstrated on real data. Citations: Martinez 1991 (Little Rock Lake); Ulanowicz et al. (Florida cypress wetlands); data from KONECT (konect.cc), see each source's terms.
- **Pandemic, CORRECTED travel model + REAL passenger volumes (nineteenth pass) — supersedes the numbers below.** Auditing the first real passenger run exposed a modelling flaw that affected every pandemic result: link weights were row-normalised after each closure, so closing an airport *redistributed* every surviving node's travel to its remaining neighbours instead of cancelling it (total mixing unchanged); with real US passenger data closures even *raised* the attack share (0.495 -> 0.543). `simulate(..., norm, reroute)` now takes each node's ORIGINAL strength: `--reroute 0` (the new default) cancels travel to closed airports, `--reroute 1` reproduces the old behaviour as a sensitivity. **Real flows**: BTS DB1B Market Q1 2023 (a 10% ticket sample, 13.4M sampled passengers, 300 US airports, 27,478 airport pairs; `data/db1b_2023_q1.zip`, `--bts`) with GeoNames populations. At k=20 closures (187 reference nodes, no intervention 0.495): bill 0.471, strength 0.471, degree 0.471, betweenness 0.472 (all tied within +/-0.001), closing the biggest metros 0.488, random 0.493. So on real passenger flows the bill is as good as the best simple rules and clearly better than random (by 0.022) and than closing the largest cities (0.016); it is not better than strength or degree. Same model on the global OpenFlights network (route counts; k=20, no intervention 0.275): bill 0.236, strength 0.230 (-0.005 +/- 0.001), degree 0.229 (-0.006 +/- 0.003), betweenness tie, random 0.272 (bill better by 0.036 +/- 0.003). With GeoNames populations (no intervention 0.263): bill 0.214, strength 0.207 (-0.007 +/- 0.001), degree 0.212 (-0.003 +/- 0.004, not significant), betweenness 0.213 (tie), biggest-metros 0.255, random 0.260 (bill better by 0.046). Under MODELLED gravity flows (sensitivity; 174 reference nodes, no intervention 0.245): bill 0.237 is barely better than random (0.241, +0.004 +/- 0.002) and among the weakest informed rules (bill/module 0.228, betweenness 0.223, strength 0.222); degree 0.208 (-0.029 +/- 0.004) is clearly best. Summary: with cancelled travel and real flows, bill ~ strength ~ degree ~ betweenness, all far better than random and than closing the biggest cities; the bill never beats degree/strength, and under the gravity assumption it is worse. Caveats: DB1B is a 10% sample of US domestic tickets only (no international), Q1 2023, itinerary markets (origin-destination demand), not flight segments, so closing an airport cancels trips that start or end there but NOT connections routed THROUGH it, which understates hub closures (a segment-level network would be needed); the SIR is a deterministic metapopulation model with one mixing parameter.
- **Pandemic LEGACY results (reroute=1: closed-airport travel redistributed; kept for the record) (real airline network, simple SIR): works, but no better than degree.** D = 5.47 (global). Paired test: 60 shared outbreak seeds, attack share measured over 197 reference nodes that no strategy closes, populations fixed from the original network, random closures drawn from a separate held-out group. Closing 20 of 300 airports cuts attack share from 0.275 to about 0.26 whatever the rule. At k=20: bill 0.263; random 0.281 (bill better by 0.018 +/- 0.003); betweenness 0.265 (tie); strength 0.260 (-0.003 +/- 0.001); degree 0.256 (-0.007 +/- 0.003). One airport per module is worse than plain bill (+0.008 +/- 0.002). Sensitivity with outbreaks seeded anywhere (hubs included): no intervention 0.332; degree 0.281, strength 0.283, betweenness 0.287, bill 0.294, bill/module 0.312, random 0.344, same ordering. Caveats: route counts not passenger volumes, saturates at mix >= 0.02. **With real populations (eleventh pass; `--experiment --population geonames`)** instead of the route-strength proxy: each airport's population is the sum of GeoNames cities >= 15,000 within 40 km (districts dropped, each city assigned to its nearest airport node; e.g. Tokyo-Haneda 27.5M, Mexico City 29M), with a new "close the biggest metros" baseline. At k=20 (179 reference nodes): no intervention 0.263; bill 0.244; betweenness 0.245 (tie, +0.000 +/- 0.002); degree 0.241 (-0.004 +/- 0.003); strength 0.238 (-0.006 +/- 0.002); random 0.269 and biggest-metros 0.272 (bill better by 0.025 and 0.028, p << 0.01). So the qualitative picture is unchanged: the bill clearly beats random closure and closing the largest cities (which is no better than random), and is tied with or slightly behind degree and strength. Population is still a catchment estimate (40 km radius is a choice), and flows are still route counts. **Flow-assumption sensitivity (sixteenth pass; `--experiment --population geonames --flows gravity`)**: real passenger volumes are not available, so link weights were replaced by MODELLED gravity flows, sqrt(pop_i * pop_j) / (1 + distance/1000 km), on the existing routes (D falls to 4.52, still global). Result at k=20 (174 reference nodes, no intervention 0.245): bill 0.250, random 0.248 (bill not better, -0.002 +/- 0.001 in random's favour), biggest-metros 0.258, strength 0.244 (-0.006 +/- 0.001), betweenness 0.251 (tie), **degree 0.230 (-0.020 +/- 0.003)**. So the finding that the bill beats random closure is NOT robust to the flow model: with gravity weights the bill picks the heaviest hubs, closing them barely changes the outcome, and plain unweighted degree is clearly the best rule. Treat the bill-vs-random result under route-count weights as conditional on that weighting. This is a modelled assumption, not data; real flows could land anywhere between.
- **Finance (synthetic core-periphery): no advantage, D adds nothing.** Bill ranks cascade damage a little better than assets (Spearman 0.51 vs 0.48; paired +0.030 +/- 0.014 SE) and degree (+0.091 +/- 0.011), but worse than plain coupling strength (0.54; paired -0.032 +/- 0.006). D never fell below 3.03 on any synthetic bank network, so the line did not separate regimes. D correlates +0.6 with contagion breadth only because both follow the capital ratio: mean coupling alone predicts breadth at +0.91. Nothing here supports "replaces stress tests". Real interbank exposures are confidential, so this is a model-class test only.

## History worth knowing

The pandemic comparison went through three versions: unpaired (noise), paired but biased (a closed node's population collapsed after closure; random closures sat inside the measured set), and the fair design above. The earlier versions gave opposite conclusions. Ecosystem/finance Spearman originally ignored ties.
